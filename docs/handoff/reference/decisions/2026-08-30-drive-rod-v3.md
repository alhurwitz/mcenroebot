## Why through the disk

`generate_reuse.py` (v2b) named its own worst property: reusing the printed disk
drops the drive onto a 3.5 mm spigot — "strictly the weaker joint." **The pin
journals the disk over 39.5 mm instead of 3.5**, so the disk cannot cock, and it
costs nothing because Ø6.0 passes through the disk's existing Ø6.5 centre hole.
v2b needed that hole opened to Ø13; this needs no drilling at all.

### The trap — M3x10 ONLY

The disk's tapped holes are recorded as "11 mm deep", measured from the disk
underside. **The first 3.5 mm of that is the recess — open space.** Only
**7.5 mm is threadable (67.9 → 75.4)**.

    M3x10  → 6.7 mm engagement.   Correct.
    M3x12  → needs 8.7 in 7.5.    Bottoms out and jacks the rod off its seat.

`check_rod_v3.py` measures the tap ceiling off the STL and asserts this.

## Glue, not press — the argument

The spline's height above the tab plane is still unknown, and it is **not**
recoverable from the STLs: the flat floor underside at 51.4 and the saddle's
body cutout do not pin down where the spline top lands.

So the socket has no opinion about it — 19.9 mm deep, open at the bottom, and
the spline stops wherever it stops. **No SPAN, no gauge rod, no fit coupon.**

A press fit cannot do that. Interference has to be *where the spline is*, and on
a 19.9 mm socket you cannot place a 10 mm interference band and know the spline
is inside it — put it at the mouth and a high spline sits in clear air above it.
A uniform Ø5.2 interference over 19.9 mm is a 20 mm press, which is the galling
failure `drive_rod_v1` was rejected for. Adhesive does not care where the spline
stops. `drive_rod_press_v3` exists only as the "5.6 feels loose on my spline"
fallback — open it with a 5.5 mm bit if it will not start.

## The nose is too long on purpose

It hangs 3.4 mm below the floor underside into the saddle cutout. Scribe rings
at z=49.0 and z=52.0. **If it fouls the servo case, saw it back — and write down
which ring.** That number is the spline height this project has never had.
Long-and-trimmable is the recoverable direction; short is a reprint. Same
principle as `generate_hub.py`'s undersized bores, applied to length.

## The disk still rides on the floor

No rod shoulder anywhere near the disk underside; the flange (Ø25.2) is smaller
than the recess (Ø26) so nothing can bear there. The Ø3 vent means the spline
cannot bottom in the socket and lift the disk either.

1. Disk upside down on the bench; rod pin-first through the centre hole, flange
   into the recess, 4× M3x10 up.
2. Drop disk + rod into the drum from above — the disk lands on the floor.
3. Adhesive on the spline; lift the servo until its tabs meet the saddle and
   screw it. The joint self-positions.
4. Look down the Ø3 vent from the disk's **top** face to confirm engagement
   before the adhesive goes off.

Print **nose down with a brim** (Ø10 footprint under a 59 mm part), 4 perimeters,
40%, no supports. The cone is exactly 45°.
